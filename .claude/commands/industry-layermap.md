Read `03-gemini-report.md` and `04-critique.md`. Use only claims the critique
tagged `hard` or `soft`; ignore `unsupported` ones.

This is the industry-track counterpart to the company `/industry-map`. The
difference: it does NOT narrow to one company or one technology. It keeps every
investable layer live and hands a RANKED set forward. Apply the value-capture
discipline from CLAUDE.md. Write `05-industry-map.md`:

1. **Layer map (the "cake").** One row per layer: what it does, representative
   players (public/private, ticker if public), margin structure, who holds
   pricing power, and **investable?** (public, liquid, a meaningful needle-mover —
   yes/no, with the reason). Distinguish your inferences from the report's claims.
2. **Profit-pool ranking.** Rank the layers by *durability of economics*, not by
   excitement. For the top 1–2: say WHY the pool stays there (what stops it
   commoditising — IP, a scarce input, switching costs, qualification cycles).
   Explicitly call out any layer where the excitement and the money diverge (a hot
   layer that is commoditising; a boring layer that is a chokepoint).
3. **Investability filter + the two routes.** Of the profit-pooling layers, which
   are reachable in public markets today vs locked in private/early-stage hands? A
   durable pool held only by private companies is a watch item, not an idea.
   Then split every reachable idea by **how much of the thesis needs the theme to
   actually become mainstream**:
   - **Route A — pure-play / high-beta.** Re-rates hard if the theme scales; if it
     stalls or commoditises you hold a cyclical/expensive asset with no floor.
   - **Route B — quality survivor + embedded option.** A cash-generative legacy
     business is the SOTP *floor*; the new theme is a cheap call on top. The more
     defensible way to own a *development-stage* theme — IF the floor is genuinely
     high-quality. Screen Route B on **floor quality** (STRONG/MEDIUM/WEAK — does
     the legacy business survive a "the theme never happens" winter?), **option
     location** (is the bet in a profit-pool/chokepoint layer or the commoditising
     one?), and **option credibility** (`hard` IP/product vs `soft` press-release).
   Do NOT gate on materiality (robotics as % of revenue): a low-materiality bet on
   a STRONG floor is the asymmetric setup; a high-materiality bet on a WEAK floor is
   a value trap. Floor quality is the screen, not size of the bet.
4. **Chokepoint layer(s) for the deep-dive.** Pick the 1–2 layers (and the
   specific technology/sub-segment within each) for `/tech-deepdive`, with a
   one-line substitution thesis each (new X replaces old Y) and a representative
   anchor player to make the structural drivers concrete. `/tech-deepdive` will
   treat that layer's chokepoint as its "subject."
5. **Candidate feed-forward.** A first-pass table of named players per investable
   layer, tagged by archetype from `01-classification.md` (cash-cow + optionality
   / pure-play / component-chokepoint / platform-software), AND by route (A/B),
   floor quality, and theme-dependence from step 3. This is the raw input
   `/industry-shortlist` will rank — do not rank or recommend here, just collect.
6. **Open questions.**
